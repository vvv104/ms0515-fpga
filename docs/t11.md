# DEC T-11 (DCT11-AA, DC310) - reference notes for the КР1807ВМ1 model

These are the facts the pin-level model of КР1807ВМ1 is built from. They come
from two DEC documents (local copies under `$MS0515_DATA/scans/t11/`):

- **ES**: *T-11 Engineering Specification*, Rev E, 1 Mar 1982
  (`T11_Engineering_Specification_Rev_E_Mar82.pdf`).
- **UG**: *T-11 User's Guide*, EK-DCT11-UG-003, 3rd ed., June 1982 (`T11_UsersMan.pdf`).

How the citations work:

- `p.N` is the page number **printed** on the page, not the PDF page. ES body
  pages are 1-33, the appendices are A-n..E-n, and the change memos at the
  front are cited as "ES memo". The ES timing diagrams (§4.3) are
  unnumbered D-size sheets, so each one is cited by its title and its PDF
  page: index p.45, refresh/IACK p.46, ASPI p.47, bus NOP p.48, DMA p.49,
  READY p.50, power-up/reset p.51, long microcycle p.52, read/write p.53.
- UG pages are printed as `chapter-page`, for example p.2-6.
- **T** is one period of the clock on XTL1/XTL0 (`T = 1/fop`, 133 ns at 7.5 MHz)
  (ES p.31 note 3).
- `L.E.` means the leading (asserting) edge of a signal and `T.E.` the
  trailing (negating) edge (ES p.31 note 3; UG Fig. 3-2).
- A text marked **[derived]** is arithmetic on figures from the documents.
  A text marked **[diagram]** is read from where an edge is drawn on a timing
  sheet. The documents give no tolerance for drawn positions; only the AC
  table bounds them.

Neither document covers КР1807ВМ1 itself. Every statement below is about
the T-11 (see `findings.md`, "КР1807ВМ1 vs. T-11").

### 1.1 SEL<1:0> (ES §2.3.14 / p.12; UG Tables 3-4, 3-5 / p.3-5)

| SEL1 | SEL0 | Static, or dynamic 64K | Dynamic 4K/16K |
|---|---|---|---|
| L | L | read, write, ASPI, bus NOP | read, write, ASPI, bus NOP |
| L | H | FETCH (instruction read) | REFRESH |
| H | L | IACK | IACK |
| H | H | DMG (DMA grant) | DMG |

- SEL<1:0> are stable before the L.E. of -RAS. A DMG can be decoded from
  SEL=3 without -RAS. Without DMA, the two SEL lines can serve as direct
  strobes. (ES p.12)
- In dynamic 64K mode, SEL0 is **not** asserted during a refresh: a 64K
  refresh shows SEL=00. (ES refresh/IACK sheet, PDF p.46, footnote; UG
  §2.11.3 / p.2-27)
- FETCH on SEL0 is asserted only during the read transaction of the fetch.
  In 8-bit mode it is asserted only during the low-byte transaction.
  (ES p.17; UG §2.5.3.2 / p.2-9)

### 1.2 Pin use per bus mode (ES §2.2.7 / p.8; UG Tables 3-2, 3-3 / p.3-3..3-4, A-4 / p.A-5)

The `x:y` form means: address bit x at the L.E. of -RAS, address bit y at
the L.E. of -CAS. The AI pins are inputs during PI.

| AI | Static (8 & 16) | 8-bit dyn 4K/16K | 8-bit dyn 64K | 16-bit dyn 4K/16K | 16-bit dyn 64K | Refresh counter bit (all dynamic) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| AI0 | -DMR | FET:A14 | A15:A14 | FET:A14 | A15:A14 | 7 (MSB) |
| AI1 | -CP3 | A1:A2 | A1:A2 | A1:A2 | A1:A2 | 0 (LSB) |
| AI2 | -CP2 | A3:A4 | A3:A4 | A3:A4 | A3:A4 | 1 |
| AI3 | -CP1 | A5:A6 | A5:A6 | A5:A6 | A5:A6 | 2 |
| AI4 | -CP0 | A7:A8 | A7:A8 | A7:A8 | A7:A8 | 3 |
| AI5 | -VEC | A9:A10 | A9:A10 | A9:A10 | A9:A10 | 4 |
| AI6 | -PF | A11:A0 | A11:A0 | A11:A12 | A11:A12 | 5 |
| AI7 | -HLT | A13:A12 | A13:A12 | A13:A14 | A13:A14 | 6 |

- **The sources disagree on 16-bit 64K, AI7 at -CAS.** ES p.8 and UG
  Tables 3-2 and A-4 give A14. UG Tables 2-4 and 2-7 (p.2-9, p.2-12) give A12.
- In static modes, the AI pins are always inputs, with low-current pull-ups
  (UG §3.3.1 / p.3-7).
- In dynamic modes, AI drive the row address before the L.E. of -RAS and the
  column address before the L.E. of -CAS. The same address is on DAL at the
  time of -RAS. (ES p.15; UG §3.3.2 / p.3-7)
- FET on AI0 (dynamic 4K/16K) is high for an instruction fetch. It is valid
  before the L.E. of -RAS and stops driving before the L.E. of PI. During a
  refresh, AI0 instead carries counter bit 7. (ES p.17; UG §2.5.3.1 / p.2-9)
- During a refresh in any dynamic mode, AI<7:0> carry the 8-bit refresh
  counter, which is **decremented** after each refresh (ES p.15).
- AI during a bus NOP or IACK: inputs in static modes, "previously latched
  data" in dynamic modes (UG §3.3 / p.3-7). The ES bus-NOP sheet (PDF p.48)
  says "asserted only in dynamic modes, driving indeterminate data".

UG Table 3-6 / A-9 (p.3-8 / p.A-7) gives what the AI pins do in each
transaction:

| Transaction | AI at -RAS L.E. | AI at -CAS L.E. | Input at PI T.E. |
|---|---|---|---|
| Read (static) | pulled up | pulled up | interrupt + DMR |
| Write (static) | pulled up | pulled up | DMR only |
| Read (dynamic) | row | column | interrupt + DMR |
| Write (dynamic) | row | column | DMR only |
| Refresh | row (counter) | - | - |
| DMA | pulled up | pulled up | DMR |
| ASPI | - | pulled up | interrupt + DMR |

### 1.3 DAL use (UG §3.2.1 / p.3-5; ES p.14)

- **Read/write:** address before -RAS, then data.
- **IACK:** DAL<12:8> carry the acknowledged request.
  - DAL<15:13> are "(0)" according to ES p.8 and "don't care" according to
    UG Table 3-1.
  - DAL<7:0> are 3-state (an input if -VEC).
- **DMA:** 3-state.
- **ASPI:**
  - UG §3.2.1 says 3-state.
  - The ES ASPI sheet (PDF p.47, note 2) says DAL<15:8> are driven in 8-bit
    mode and 3-state in 16-bit mode.
- **Bus NOP and refresh:**
  - ES p.14 says "indeterminate data".
  - UG §3.2.1 says "previously latched data".
- **16-bit byte write:** the byte not addressed drives indeterminate data
  (ES read/write sheet, PDF p.53, note 1).
- **UG Fig. A-16, note 8** (p.A-52): the DALs are always driven except during
  DMA and the data portion of a read.

### 1.4 Pull-ups (ES §3.2 / p.23; UG Table A-2 / p.A-2)

- Low-current pull-ups exist on AI<7:0>, READY and some DAL lines. The
  DC tables give "DAL<15:7,2:0>".
- **The sources disagree on which DAL pull-ups are enabled while -BCLR is low:**
  - ES §2.2 / p.5, §2.3.17 / p.14 and the power-up sheet (PDF p.51, note 1):
    DAL<15:8> and DAL<2:0>.
  - UG §3.5.1 / p.3-13 and §4.4 / p.4-4: DAL<15:8> and DAL<1:0>.
- In every mode, the AI pins get low-current pull-ups during DMG (ES p.15).

## 2. Clock

- **Oscillator.**
  - Crystal: fundamental mode, 0.1-7.5 MHz (ES §4.1 / p.25, §4.2 / p.33).
  - External TTL clock: drive XTL1 and ground XTL0. It needs T ≥ 133 ns,
    rise/fall < 80 ns and high/low > 60 ns (UG §5.4.2 / p.5-4).
  - The part is static: with an external clock the minimum frequency is DC
    (ES p.32 note 11). XTL1 may be gated or held high/low indefinitely
    (UG §5.4.2 / p.5-4).
- **Phases.** The basic unit is the *clock phase*, and all phases are equally
  long (UG §2.2.3 / p.2-2). One phase is one XTL period T: a microcycle is
  "3 or 4 oscillator periods" (ES §2.2.6 / p.7), and ES App. B notes 3
  phases per microcycle (ES p.B-7 note 0).
- **Microcycle.**
  - A standard microcycle is three phases, φ1, φ2 and φW.
  - A fourth phase, φD, is inserted between φ2 and φW in ASPI, IACK and DMA
    transactions, and in every microcycle in long-microcycle mode
    (UG §2.2.3 / p.2-2).
  - The ES refresh sheet (PDF p.46) also draws φD in the refresh cycle. ES
    App. D charges one extra period per refresh (ES p.D-2 formula).
  - So a microcycle is 3T, or 4T when it contains φD.
- **Transaction.** A transaction is one or two microcycles (UG §2.2.2 /
  p.2-2). A microcycle is "the activity required for one microinstruction".
- **The documents' word for a state is "phase".** No smaller internal state
  is documented.
- **Where a microcycle begins.** The UG lists the phases as φ1, φ2, φW. The ES
  timing sheets draw every transaction from the start of φ2 to the end of φ1:
  - read/write, PDF p.53;
  - bus NOP, PDF p.48: one NOP spans φ2, φW, φ1;
  - refresh, PDF p.46: SEL0 spans φ2, φD, φW, φ1.
- **COUT, pulse mode (MR0=1, PMC):** one pulse per microcycle, in φW
  (UG §4.3.1 / p.4-4).
  - Pulse width ≥ T-33 = 100 ns (tOPW).
  - Low time ≥ 2T-37 = 230 ns (tORT), or ≥ 3T-37 = 367 ns when φD is present
    (tORD).
  - (ES p.28)
- **COUT, constant mode (MR0=0):** fosc/2, a square wave of two periods.
  Its polarity against the phases depends on where φD occurs
  (UG Fig. 3-6 / p.3-16; ES long-microcycle sheet, PDF p.52).
- **COUT at power-up.**
  - A high level on PUP initializes MR0=1 (ES §2.3.7 / p.10).
  - COUT starts at most T+60 = 193 ns after the T.E. of PUP (tPCO; ES p.29).
  - The power-up sheet (PDF p.51) draws COUT stopped low while PUP is high,
    from tPUT on.
  - [diagram] UG Fig. A-16 (p.A-52) labels COUT first "osc. with mode reg(0)=1",
    then "per mode bit".
- **COUT and the bus.**
  - Pulse-mode COUT L.E. is at or before the L.E. of -RAS: tMOR ≥ 0.
  - COUT T.E. is ≥ T-51 = 82 ns after the L.E. of -RAS (tMRO).
  - The L.E. of -CAS is ≥ 10 ns after the T.E. of COUT (tOSC).
  - So [derived] in a read/write, -RAS falls at the start of φW, together with
    the COUT pulse, and -CAS falls just after φW ends.
  - (ES p.27-28)

## 3. Mode register

The mode register (MR) is loaded from DAL<15:0> while -BCLR is low, at
power-up and on the RESET instruction. MR bit n is DAL bit n. Undriven bits
read as 1 because of the pull-ups. It cannot be accessed at any other time.
(ES §2.2 / p.5; UG §1.2.3 / p.1-4, §4.4 / p.4-4)

| Bit | Name | 1 (H, also the pull-up default) | 0 (L) | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 15:13 | start/restart | see table below | | ES §2.2.5 / p.7 |
| 12 | -TESTER | user | tester (DEC only) | ES §2.2.3 / p.7 |
| 11 | -MD16 | 8-bit data bus | 16-bit data bus | ES §2.2.2 / p.6 |
| 10 | -MD64K | 4K/16K dynamic RAM | 64K dynamic RAM (no effect in static mode) | ES p.6; UG §4.2.3 / p.4-3 |
| 9 | -DYNAMIC | static | dynamic (row/column on AI, refresh) | ES p.6 |
| 8 | -NORM | delayed R/W | normal R/W | ES p.6; UG §4.3.3 / p.4-4 |
| 7:2 | - | reserved | reserved | ES p.5 |
| 1 | -LMC | standard microcycle | long microcycle (every microcycle +1 phase = φD) | ES §2.2.4 / p.7; UG §4.3.2 / p.4-4 |
| 0 | PMC | COUT once per microcycle | COUT = fosc/2 | ES §2.2.6 / p.7 |

**Bus-mode details** (ES p.6; UG §4.2, §4.3):

- **Delayed (MR8=1):** -RD/-WT (8-bit) or R/-WHB, R/-WLB (16-bit) become
  valid after the assertion of -RAS. They have the same timing as -CAS
  (UG §4.3.3). In 8-bit mode the bus is then a subset of the 8085 bus.
- **Normal (MR8=0):** the R/W signals are stable before the L.E. of -RAS and
  stay valid after its T.E. (6800-compatible).
- **16-bit mode:** one transaction per word. R/-WHB and R/-WLB act as
  separate byte write enables.
- **8-bit mode:** a word takes two transactions, low byte (A0=0) first and
  high byte (A0=1) second. They are indivisible. -RD and -WT are mutually
  exclusive. (UG §2.7 / p.2-13, §4.2.1.2 / p.4-2)

**Tester mode (MR12=0)** (ES §2.2.3 / p.7):

- Before every instruction fetch, the chip performs an extra WRITE cycle.
- The write address is "0, PS<7:0>". In 8-bit mode, the low byte goes to
  that address and the high byte to the same address with its low bit
  forced to 1.
- The data written are the destination register of the last instruction.

**Start/restart addresses** (ES §2.2.5 / p.7; UG Fig. 1-4 / p.1-4, Table B-4 / p.B-11):

| MR15:13 | START (power-up PC) | RESTART (HALT) |
|---|---|---|
| 111 | 172000 | 172004 |
| 110 | 173000 | 173004 |
| 101 | 000000 | 000004 |
| 100 | 010000 | 010004 |
| 011 | 020000 | 020004 |
| 010 | 040000 | 040004 |
| 001 | 100000 | 100004 |
| 000 | 140000 | 140004 |

- START is used only at power-up. RESET reloads the MR but does **not**
  load the PC (UG p.B-11).
- RESTART is used by the HALT instruction and by the -HALT input
  (ES p.7).

**PUP forces two MR bits:**

- PUP high initializes MR0=1 (ES p.10).
- PUP high forces MR1 to "normal microcycle", and leaves every other
  register, R0-R7 included, indeterminate (ES p.32 note 7).

**When the MR is read** (details in §4):

- Mode input begins several microcycles after -BCLR is asserted with PUP
  low. It continues until the microcode returns -BCLR high (ES §2.3.8 /
  p.10; UG §3.5.2.3 / p.3-14).
- While -BCLR is low, the DALs map one-for-one onto the MR. When -BCLR is
  negated, the MR is write-protected (UG §5.3 / p.5-1).
- UG Fig. A-16 (p.A-52) draws nine separate windows, "mode input 1" to
  "mode input 9". The RESET sequence has a 3-transaction group repeated nine
  times while -BCLR is low (UG Table A-3 note 3 / p.A-4). [derived] One mode
  sample per group is plausible; the documents do not say it.
- The mode data must stay stable for the whole -BCLR pulse (UG §4.4).
  - Hold after the T.E. of -BCLR: tMHB ≥ 0 (ES p.27).
  - DAL valid ≤ 74T after the T.E. of PUP (tPMU), or ≤ 74T after the L.E. of
    -BCLR on RESET (tBMR) (ES p.25, p.29).

## 4. Power-up and reset

### 4.1 PUP (ES §2.3.4 / p.9; UG §3.5.2 / p.3-13; ES power-up sheet PDF p.51; UG Fig. A-16 / p.A-52)

**While PUP is high:**

- Internal cycles are forced to an initial state and register contents are
  undefined.
- -BCLR is asserted, at most 100 ns after the L.E. of PUP (tPBC).
- Within 250 ns of the L.E. of PUP (tPUT), the output pins are preset:
  - -RAS, -CAS, PI, SEL0, SEL1, R/-WHB and R/-WLB are unasserted;
  - low-current pull-ups are on AI<7:0> and on the mode DALs.
- If the mode input violates the DAL tristate time, those controls are
  undefined during -BCLR (ES sheet note 4).
- UG Fig. A-16 note 9: during -BCLR at power-up, control signals may assert
  before the chip reads the MR.
- PUP raised during operation terminates the current transaction
  (UG §3.5.2).
- Minimum PUP high time: tUPP = 100 000 ns (ES p.30). PUP thresholds are
  given in UG Table A-2 / p.A-3 and ES p.24, and differ between them (see
  the conflicts table).

**Sequence after the falling edge of PUP:**

| Step | Event | Timing (from PUP T.E.) | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Internal clock starts; COUT runs | COUT L.E. ≤ T+60 (tPCO) | ES p.9, p.29 |
| 2 | MR read from DAL for "several microcycles" | mode bits valid ≤ 74T (tPMU), held to -BCLR T.E. (tMHB ≥ 0) | ES p.9, p.27, p.29 |
| 3 | -BCLR negated (high) | 99T min, 100T max (tPBU) | ES p.28 |
| 4 | 10 refresh transactions (16-bit dynamic) or 20 (8-bit dynamic); in static mode, 10 or 20 bus NOPs | - | UG §3.5.2 / p.3-13, Table 3-8 / p.3-15; ES says "several refresh cycles" |
| 5 | SP ← 000376, PC ← START, PSW ← 000340 (UG order; ES lists PC, PSW, SP) | - | UG §3.5.2.5; ES p.9 |
| 6 | One ASPI. It checks DMR and HALT, since the other requests are masked by PSW=340 (ES); UG says interrupts and DMA are serviced before the first fetch | - | ES p.9; UG Fig. A-16 note 5 |
| 7 | First instruction fetch begins | 295T min, 315T max (tPFF) | ES p.29 |

Further notes on the sequence:

- UG Fig. A-16 note 6 is attached to AI<7:0> between the power-up
  refreshes: AI are "asserted in dynamic mode only, reflects contents of the
  mode register". This is as printed; what is driven is not given.
- Supply: the chip is undefined until VCC is stable, whatever PUP does
  (UG §5.2 / p.5-1). A capacitor from PUP to VCC is the simplest power-up
  circuit (ES p.9).

### 4.2 RESET instruction (UG §6.3.6 / p.6-62, §B.3.2.2 / p.B-6; ES reset sheet PDF p.51)

- **What it does:** asserts -BCLR, reloads the MR, negates -BCLR, then runs
  one ASPI. R0-R7, PS and the condition codes are unchanged. The PC is not
  reloaded with START.
- **-BCLR width:**
  - ES p.25 (tBCR) gives ≥ 84T. [derived] That is 11.2 µs at 7.5 MHz.
  - UG p.B-6 and ES p.C-3 give "minimum 8.4 µs", followed by a pause of at
    least 150 ns.
- **Mode bits:** valid ≤ 74T after the L.E. of -BCLR (tBMR).
- **DAL release:** DAL<15:0> float ≤ 150 ns after the L.E. of -BCLR (tDFB).
  tDFB may be violated on DAL<15:8,1:0> only; a violation on DAL<7:2> "may
  corrupt the processor" (ES p.32 note 10; ES memo).
- **COUT** switches from the old mode to the new one while -BCLR is low
  (ES PDF p.51).
- **Bus sequence** (UG Table A-3 / p.A-4):
  - 16-bit: `R Ref N N N N [N N N]x9 N N N A N`.
  - 8-bit: `R-R Ref N N N N [N N N]x9 N N N A N`.
  - -BCLR is low during the bracketed part.
  - The total is 39 microcycles (16-bit) and 41 (8-bit) (ES p.B-6).
- **Power fail during RESET** is not recognized until the instruction ends
  (ES p.C-3; UG p.B-6).

## 5. Bus transactions

### 5.0 Kinds and their length (UG §2.2.1 / p.2-2, Table 3-7 / p.3-9, Table A-10 / p.A-8; ES sheets)

| Transaction | Microcycles [diagram] | Strobes asserted | SEL<1:0> | READY slips |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Read (DATI) | 2 × 3T ("read address" + "read input"; UG Fig. A-4) | -RAS, -CAS, PI; -RD in 8-bit | 00, or 01 for a fetch (static, 64K) | yes |
| Write (DATO) | 2 × 3T | -RAS, -CAS, PI; R/-WxB or -WT | 00 | yes |
| Refresh | 1 × 4T (φ2 φD φW φ1) | -RAS only | 01 (4K/16K), 00 (64K) | no |
| IACK | 1 × 4T (φ2 φD φW φ1) | -RAS only | 10 | yes |
| DMA (DMG) | 2 × 4T (φD in each) | -RAS, -CAS, PI | 11 | yes |
| ASPI | 1 microcycle with φD | -CAS, PI (no -RAS) | 00 | not listed (UG Table 3-7) |
| Bus NOP | 1 × 3T | none | 00 | - |

- In long-microcycle mode, every microcycle is 4T (UG §4.3.2).
- Each READY pulse adds 3T (4T in long mode) (ES p.31 note 4).
- "R-W" (read-modify-write) is a read followed by a write to the same
  address, and the pair is indivisible:
  - Every DATO is preceded by a DATI of the same location, except stack
    writes during interrupts and traps (UG §2.4.1 / p.2-4).
  - ES p.12 phrases the exception as "except for stack operations".
  - No DMG can be issued between the DATI and the DATO (ES p.12).
  - The rule covers MOV, CLR and SXT to memory (UG §B.4 / p.B-8; ES
    p.C-6 items 26, 35; ES p.C-7 item 44).

### 5.1 16-bit read and write, dynamic (the MS 0515 case) - phase by phase

The sources for this subsection are:

- the ES read/write sheet (PDF p.53), drawn with pulse-mode COUT;
- UG Fig. A-4 (16-bit dynamic read, p.A-28) and Fig. A-5 (16-bit dynamic
  write);
- the edges, which are taken from the drawings [diagram];
- the bounds, which come from the AC table (§5.8).

Normal microcycles are assumed: no φD, no READY.

**Microcycle A, "address"** (the same for read and write):

| Phase | Event |
|---|---|
| φ2 | DAL<15:0> = address. AI = row address; in 4K/16K, AI0 = FET. SEL<1:0> valid: FETCH, if any, goes high here. Normal mode: R/-WxB already at their final level (low for a write). |
| φW | COUT pulse (pulse mode). -RAS falls at the start of φW. After the row hold, AI switch to the column address. |
| φ1 | -CAS falls just after φW. Read: DAL float at the L.E. of -CAS (tDFC ≥ 0). Write: write data appear ≤ 80 ns after the L.E. of -CAS (tCWD). Delayed mode: R/-WxB (16-bit write) or -RD/-WT (8-bit) assert together with -CAS. |

**Microcycle B, "data"**:

| Phase | Read | Write |
|---|---|---|
| φ2 | PI rises. The column address floats at the L.E. of PI (tAFP ≥ 0), and AI become inputs. SEL0 (fetch) falls at about the L.E. of PI. | PI rises. Write data are valid ≥ T-83 before the L.E. of PI (tWDP). |
| φW | COUT pulse. Read data must be valid by now (bounds below). | COUT pulse. AI are sampled for DMR only; interrupts are not accepted during a write (ES PDF p.53 note 6). |
| φ1 | -CAS rises: **data are latched at the T.E. of -CAS** (tRDC hold 0). PI falls: AI requests are latched at the T.E. of PI (tIHP hold 0). -RAS rises last. | -CAS rises, PI falls, -RAS rises. Data stay held after each edge (tWHC, tWHP, tWHR). Normal mode: R/-WxB rise after the T.E. of -RAS (tNHR). Delayed mode: they rise with -CAS. |

After microcycle B:

- the next address may be driven ≥ T-18 after the T.E. of -CAS (tCDE) and
  ≥ T-118 after the T.E. of -RAS (tRDE);
- AI drive the next address ≥ T-40 after the T.E. of PI (tPAE).

[derived] A back-to-back read is 6T. This agrees with the precharge minima:

|---|---|---|
| -RAS | 2T-120 | about 2 phases |
| -CAS | 3T-5 | about 3 phases |
| PI | 4T-33 | about 4 phases |

### 5.2 16-bit static

Static mode works as in §5.1 except for the address pins (UG §2.3-2.4,
Figs. A-2, A-3):

- AI never drive an address; they are inputs with pull-ups throughout.
- A fetch is shown by SEL=01.

### 5.3 8-bit modes (UG §2.7-2.10 / p.2-13..2-24)

- SAL<15:8> hold the high address byte for the whole transaction.
- DAL<7:0> carry the low address byte, then the data byte.
- A word is two transactions: the low byte (A0=0), then the high byte
  (A0=1). They are indivisible. A read-modify-write of a word is
  `R-R-W-W`.
- -RD (pin 27) is asserted for a read and -WT (pin 28) for a write.
  - Normal mode: valid before the L.E. of -RAS.
  - Delayed mode: they have the timing of -CAS (UG Tables 2-12, 2-14, 2-18,
    2-23).
- 8-bit dynamic AI mapping: see §1.2.
- 64K and static modes signal a fetch on SEL0 during the low-byte
  transaction only. 4K/16K signals it on AI0.

### 5.4 Refresh (ES refresh sheet, PDF p.46; UG §2.11 / p.2-25)

The refresh transaction is one microcycle with φD:

| Phase | Event |
|---|---|
| φ2 | AI = counter, with tASR ≥ T-83 before the L.E. of -RAS. SEL0 rises in 4K/16K (tFFR ≥ T-23 before the L.E. of -RAS); it stays 0 in 64K. |
| φD | -RAS falls. |
| φW | - |
| φ1 | -RAS rises (width ≥ 2T+35; tFRP). AI change before the T.E. of -RAS (tAHR ≥ T-60). SEL0 falls ≥ T-123 after the T.E. of -RAS (tSHF). SEL0 width ≥ 4T-20 (tFSP). |

- -CAS, PI and SEL1 are not asserted. DAL carry indeterminate data.
- If the next microcycle is a refresh, SEL0 stays set (ES DMA sheet note 1).
- **When a refresh happens:**
  - ES p.13: in 8-bit dynamic mode, after every instruction fetch, once per
    source or destination operand fetch in mode 5, 6 or 7, and twice more in
    any trap sequence. In 16-bit mode, every other such opportunity.
  - UG §2.11.1 / p.2-25: after a fetch (8-bit: every instruction; 16-bit:
    every other one), after modes 5, 6, 7, during HALT, TRAP, BPT and IOT,
    and during all interrupts and traps.
- **Rate:** the worst case is 128 refreshes in 1.33 ms at 7.5 MHz (ES p.13).
  App. D gives average refreshes per instruction (ES p.D-2):

  | Instruction | Modes | 16-bit | 8-bit |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | SOP, XOR, JMP, JSR | dst 0-4 | 0.5 | 1 |
  | SOP, XOR, JMP, JSR | dst 5-7 | 1.0 | 2 |
  | DOP | src 0-4, dst 0-4 | 0.5 | 1 |
  | DOP | src 0-4, dst 5-7 | 1.0 | 2 |
  | DOP | src 5-7, dst 0-4 | 1.0 | 2 |
  | DOP | src 5-7, dst 5-7 | 1.5 | 3 |
  | EMT, TRAP, BPT, IOT | the column printed "0-4" | 1.5 | 3 |
  | EMT, TRAP, BPT, IOT | the column printed "5-7" | 0.5 | 1 |

- **Cost of a refresh:** one extra period T over the static timing (ES App.
  D formula: `f = (N/X)·(3·A/R + 1)`). In ES App. A, "refresh on" is at most
  one T longer per refresh than "refresh off" (ES p.A-2..A-7). [derived]
  Static times = App. B microcycles × 3T. Dynamic adds T for each refresh
  actually performed: the refresh microcycle is 4T where a bus NOP would be
  3T.
- **Counter:** 8 bits, decremented after each refresh; its bit order on AI
  is in §1.2 (ES p.15).

### 5.5 IACK (ES refresh/IACK sheet, PDF p.46; UG §2.12 / p.2-27)

The IACK transaction is one microcycle with φD. **No -CAS and no PI.**

| Phase | Event |
|---|---|
| φ2 | SEL1 rises (tKKR ≥ T-63 before the L.E. of -RAS). DAL<12:8> = the request being acknowledged (tDSR ≥ T-48 before the L.E. of -RAS). DAL<7:0> 3-state. |
| φD | -RAS falls. The L.E. of -RAS strobes the acknowledge data to the system (UG §3.4.1). |
| φW | External vector input on DAL<7:2>. It must be valid ≤ 2T-148 after the L.E. of -RAS (tKRD) and ≤ 3T-155 after the L.E. of SEL1 (tKSD). |
| φ1 | SEL1 falls, and the **vector is latched** (tKDS hold 0). -RAS rises ≥ 45 ns later (tRHS). -RAS width ≥ 2T+35 (tKRP). DAL<15:8> held ≥ T-50 after the T.E. of SEL1 (tKHS). |

- **Disagreement on the vector strobe:** UG §2.12.2 says the vector is
  latched at the T.E. of SEL1, and UG §3.4.1 says at the T.E. of -RAS. The
  ES gives its hold time against SEL1.
- SEL1 width ≥ 3T-66 (tKSP). SEL1 recovery ≥ T (tSPR).
- READY delays the vector latch by one microcycle per pulse (UG §2.12.2).
- An IACK is issued only for a CP request: neither -PF nor -HALT is
  acknowledged (ES p.16).

### 5.6 DMA (ES DMA sheet, PDF p.49; ES p.17; UG §1.6, §2.14 / p.2-29)

- **Request:** -DMR (AI0 low) is read at **every** PI, write transactions
  included (UG §1.6 / p.1-8).
- **When it is granted:** at the end of the current transaction (read,
  write, DMG or ASPI), except between the DATI and DATO of a
  read-modify-write.
- **The grant, SEL=11:**
  - DAL<15:0> go 3-state (tDFS ≥ 0 before the L.E. of SEL0).
  - AI<7:0>, R/-WHB and R/-WLB get low-current pull-ups (tRSD ≥ 0 before
    the L.E. of SEL0).
  - The CPU keeps driving -RAS, -CAS, PI and COUT with read/write-like
    timing.
  - The external master drives the address, the AI row/column multiplexing,
    R/-WxB and the data.
  - In pulse mode, the T.E. of COUT marks the time to switch AI from row to
    column.
- **[diagram] Shape of one DMA transaction:** φ2 φD φW φ1 φ2 φD φW φ1 (8T).
  - SEL0 and SEL1 rise in φ2.
  - -RAS falls at the start of φW; SEL0 L.E. ≥ 2T-23 before it (tSFR) and
    SEL1 L.E. ≥ 2T-63 before it (tSKR).
  - -CAS falls ≥ 2T+10 after the L.E. of -RAS (tMRC), in the next φ2.
  - PI rises ≥ T-28 after the L.E. of -CAS.
  - -CAS, PI, SEL1 and -RAS end in the last φ1.
  - Widths: -RAS ≥ 5T+35 (tMRP); SEL0 ≥ 8T-38 (tMSF); SEL1 ≥ 7T-68 (tMSK).
    Add 4T per READY pulse and 8T if several DMA cycles are granted.
  - The DALs and R/W are re-enabled ≥ T-27 after the T.E. of SEL1 (tDSE,
    tRSE).
- **Continuation:** the grant repeats while AI0 is low at the T.E. of each PI
  (UG §2.14). One READY pulse extends a DMA transaction by one microcycle.

### 5.7 ASPI and bus NOP

**ASPI** (ES ASPI sheet, PDF p.47; ES p.13; UG §2.15 / p.2-31):

- -CAS falls at the start of φ2.
- PI rises at the start of φD (tCSP ≥ T-28 after -CAS).
- COUT pulses in φW.
- -CAS and PI end at φ1.
- Requests are latched at the T.E. of PI.
- -RAS stays high, so external decoders must AND -CAS with -RAS
  (UG §5.5 / p.5-5).
- **When ASPI occurs:**
  - ES p.13 and sheet note: before the first fetch, during WAIT, and after
    RESET.
  - UG §2.15: during RESET, HALT (instruction or interrupt), WAIT and
    power-up.

**Bus NOP** (ES PDF p.48; UG §2.13 / p.2-29): one 3T microcycle with no bus
activity, used for internal operations such as decode and arithmetic.

| Signal | Level |
|---|---|
| -RAS, -CAS | high |
| PI | low |
| R/-WHB, R/-WLB | high |
| SEL1, SEL0 | low |
| DAL | indeterminate (ES) or previously latched (UG) |
| AI | indeterminate in dynamic mode |

## 6. READY (ES §2.3.15 / p.13; ES p.32 notes 5-6; ES READY sheet PDF p.50; UG §3.4.6 / p.3-11, Fig. 3-3)

- READY is edge-triggered and normally activated by driving its pin low.
- It is also activated internally by the L.E. of -RAS if the pin is
  already low at that point. So READY tied to GND gives exactly one slip on
  every I/O or IACK transaction.
- A 400 ns minimum idle can be guaranteed by gating -RAS with the device's
  chip select onto READY.
- One assertion gives one slip of one microcycle (3T, or 4T in long mode).
  Each further slip needs a new pulse. The ES READY sheet (note 3) toggles
  READY during φW, gated with COUT.
- READY has no effect unless -RAS is asserted (UG §3.4.6).
- **Where slips can occur:**
  - In DATI, DATO, IACK and DMG, but not in refresh (ES p.13).
  - UG Table 3-7 lists read/write, IACK and DMG, but not refresh or ASPI.
- **Where the slip goes:**
  - UG Fig. 3-3 says the slip starts after the leading edges of -RAS, -CAS
    and PI.
  - [diagram] On the ES READY sheet, the slip lengthens the data microcycle:
    -CAS, PI and -RAS are all held one microcycle longer, and the
    read-data and interrupt windows move one microcycle later.
- **When READY must arrive:**
  - Its L.E. must come ≤ 3T-100 after the L.E. of -RAS (tYSR), ≤ 2T-135
    after the L.E. of -CAS (tYSC), or ≤ 2T-127 after the T.E. of pulse COUT
    (tYSO).
  - Width ≥ 60 ns. For multiple slips, recovery ≥ 60 ns and hold from the
    T.E. of COUT ≥ 0.
  - UG Fig. A-15's table (p.A-51) names the edge "READY (T.E.)" for tYSC and
    tYSR. The ES Rev E change memo corrected this to L.E.
- **Effect on timing:** ES App. A adds 0.4 µs (= 3T) per READY pulse
  (ES p.A-1 note 2).

## 7. Interrupts, traps, HALT

### 7.1 Request encoding (ES §2.1 / p.4; UG Table 1-2 / p.1-7, Table A-1 / p.A-1)

The requests are sampled on AI while PI is high and are active low.

| -CP3 | -CP2 | -CP1 | -CP0 | Level | Vector |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| L | L | L | L | 7 | 140 |
| L | L | L | H | 7 | 144 |
| L | L | H | L | 7 | 150 |
| L | L | H | H | 7 | 154 |
| L | H | L | L | 6 | 100 |
| L | H | L | H | 6 | 104 |
| L | H | H | L | 6 | 110 |
| L | H | H | H | 6 | 114 |

| H | H | H | L | 4 | 70 |
| H | H | H | H | - | no request |

- **-HLT:** level 8, no vector. It pushes PS and PC, then PS ← 340 and
  PC ← RESTART.
- **-PF:** vector 24/26. **Its level is 7 in ES p.4 and 8 in UG Tables 1-2
  and A-1.** ES p.9 also says PSW=340 masks everything except DMR and HALT.
- **-DMR:** listed by ES as level 8; it is not an interrupt (§5.6).
- **Masking:** a CP request is taken if its level is greater than PSW<7:5>
  (UG p.B-9). A request that is not removed after its IACK, served with a
  new PS of lower priority, re-interrupts until the stack overflows
  (UG §1.5.5.1).
- **-VEC** (AI5) matters only together with a CP request. The CP priority
  still applies, but the vector comes from DAL<7:2> during the IACK, with
  bits 1:0 = 0 (ES p.16; UG §3.2.3). An -VEC acknowledge drives DAL12 low.
- **Default vector:** UG Table A-14 / p.A-11 says the vector is 0 if the
  device fails to drive the DALs. ES p.C-7 item 46 says vector addresses
  are limited to 4..374.

### 7.2 When requests are sampled and taken

- CP, -VEC, -PF and -HLT are read at the T.E. of PI during **read and ASPI**
  transactions only, not during writes or DMA. -DMR is read at every PI.
  (UG §1.5.1 / p.1-5, §3.3.3 / p.3-8; ES p.15-16 "except during DATA OUT")
- The latency has three parts (ES p.B-7..B-8):
  1. the request waits for a PI to latch it, possibly in the next
     instruction;
  2. the instruction that latched it runs to completion;
  3. the interrupt microcode runs.

  No action is taken until the end of the current instruction; priority is
  arbitrated before the next fetch (UG §1.5.1).
- **The interrupt sequence** (UG Table A-3 / p.A-4), where `R-W` is the end
  of the interrupted instruction:
  - 16-bit: `... R-W [I N N* N W Ref N W R R N] R(first fetch of the service routine)`.
  - 8-bit: `... R-R-W-W [I N N* N W-W Ref N W-W R-R R N] R-R`.
  - `N*` is absent with an internal vector.
  - [derived] The order matches PS pushed, then PC pushed, then the two
    vector words read. In 8-bit mode the new PS is read as a single byte.
  - Which vector word is read first is not stated.
- **Maximum latency** (ES p.B-7, p.A-8; UG Table A-22 / p.A-20). The worst
  case for interrupts is a request during JSR (mode 2 or 4) or EMT; for DMR,
  during MTPS mode 0. These are microcycles + extra phases + µs, at 7.5 MHz:

  | Request | 16-bit static | 16-bit dynamic | 8-bit static | 8-bit dynamic |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | CP, PF, internal vector | 38 µc (15.20) | 38 µc + 2T (15.47) | 54 (21.60) | 54 + 4T (22.13) |
  | CP + VEC, external vector | 39 (15.60) | 39 + 2T (15.87) | 55 (22.00) | 55 + 4T (22.53) |
  | DMR | 8 + 1T + 0.19 µs (3.52) | 8 + 2T + 0.19 (3.66) | 10 + 1T + 0.19 (4.32) | 10 + 2T + 0.19 (4.46) |
  | in WAIT, internal vector | 19 + 1T (7.73) | 19 + 2T (7.87) | 25 + 1T (10.13) | 25 + 4T (10.53) |
  | in WAIT, external vector | 20 + 1T (8.13) | 20 + 2T (8.27) | 26 + 1T (10.53) | 26 + 4T (10.93) |
  | in WAIT, DMR | 3 + 2T + 0.19 (1.66) | 3 + 2T + 0.19 (1.66) | 3 + 2T + 0.19 (1.66) | 3 + 3T + 0.19 (1.79) |

- **-PF** (ES p.16; UG §3.3.3 / p.3-9):
  - It needs no setup time: the chip samples it, then waits up to one
    instruction before recognizing it.
  - It is pseudo-edge-sensitive: it must be read negated once before
    another assertion counts.
  - There is no IACK. It traps through 24, then 26.
  - If -PF and a level-7 CP request arrive at the same PI, -PF is served
    first.
  - UG §6.3.5.9 also says, as printed, "a trap will occur if an RTI
    instruction is executed in a power-fail service routine".
- **-HALT** (ES p.16; UG §3.3.3):
  - It is pseudo-edge-sensitive and not maskable, with no IACK.
  - It pushes PS and PC, then PS ← 340 and PC ← RESTART.
- **The T bit** (UG §6.3.5.8, §B.3.4 / p.B-7; ES memo, item 11A):
  - The trace trap goes through vector 14, at the end of the instruction
    after the one that set T.
  - T can be set only by RTI, RTT, a trap or an interrupt; MTPS cannot
    set it.
  - If RTI sets T, the trap is taken before the next instruction. If RTT
    sets T, it is taken after the next instruction.
  - The T trap is taken before a pending interrupt. While T is set, no
    interrupt requests are serviced, and inside the T service routine other
    interrupts are blocked.
  - With T set, WAIT is exited.
- **Priority between simultaneous events — the sources disagree:**
  - UG §6.3.5.11 / p.6-61: HALT > trace > PF > CP > instruction traps.
  - UG §B.5 / p.B-8: HALT > PF > trace > CP7 > CP6 > CP5 > CP4 > trap
    instructions.
  - ES p.C-8 lists, for the T11 and without a stated order: TRAP
    instructions, HALT interrupt, trace trap, external-vector interrupt,
    internal-vector interrupt, power-fail trap, WAIT loop, TEST mode
    request.
- **First instruction of a handler:** if a higher-priority interrupt
  arrives, the first instruction of an interrupt routine is not executed
  (ES p.C-4 item 16).

- A trap pushes PS, then PC, then sets PC ← (vector) and PS ← (vector+2)

- **There are no bus errors** (UG §B.5.1 / p.B-9; ES p.C-5 items 21, 27-29;
  ES §1.4 p.3):
  - no bus timeout;
  - no odd-address trap: for a word access at an odd address, the LSB is
    ignored and the word at the even address is used;
  - no stack-overflow trap;
  - a read of non-existent memory completes and returns whatever is on DAL,
    and a write to it is lost;
  - odd or non-existent SP references do not trap (ES p.C-4 item 15);
  - an instruction runs to completion regardless (ES p.C-7 item 45).

### 7.4 HALT (instruction and input)

- The T-11 has no console. HALT behaves like a trap: it pushes PS, then PC,
  then sets PS ← 340 and PC ← RESTART (START + 4). It does not stop the
  processor. (UG §B.3.2.1 / p.B-6; ES p.C-7 item 42; ES §1.4)
- **ES App. E note 1 (p.E-6) instead says the PC is written to location 4
  and the PS to location 6.** Every other place in both documents says "the
  stack".
- **Bus sequence** (UG Table A-3):
  - 16-bit: `R Ref N N W Ref N W N N A N`.
  - 8-bit: `R-R Ref N N W-W Ref N W-W N N A N`.
  - There is an ASPI (`A`) near the end.

## 8. DMA

See §5.6. In summary:

- The request is AI0 low during any PI.
- The grant is SEL=11, after the current transaction but never inside a
  read-modify-write.
- During the grant:
  - DAL go 3-state;
  - AI, R/-WHB and R/-WLB get weak pull-ups;
  - the CPU keeps pulsing -RAS, -CAS, PI and COUT, one DMA transaction
    (two microcycles with φD) at a time.
- The grant continues while AI0 is low at the T.E. of each PI
  (UG §1.6 / p.1-8, §5.8 / p.5-17).
- Latency: see §7.2.
- In UG's example circuit, a write to a DMA control register can raise
  DMR, and the request is latched during that same write transaction
  (UG §5.8.2 / p.5-19).

## 9. Instruction set: T-11 against the PDP-11

**What it implements:** the LSI-11 instruction set less MARK (ES §1.4 / p.3).
ES §2.6 / p.22 lists:

- **Single operand:** CLR(B), COM(B), INC(B), DEC(B), NEG(B), ADC(B), SBC(B),
  TST(B), ROR(B), ROL(B), ASR(B), ASL(B), SWAB, SXT, MTPS, MFPS.
- **Double operand:** MOV(B), CMP(B), BIT(B), BIC(B), BIS(B), ADD, SUB, XOR.
- **Program control:** Bxx, SOB, JMP, JSR, RTS, RTI, RTT, EMT, TRAP, BPT,
  IOT.
- **Miscellaneous:** HALT, WAIT, RESET, NOP, condition-code operators,
  **MFPT**.

Per-instruction semantics follow UG ch. 6 (standard PDP-11) and are not
repeated here. The opcode map is UG Table A-13 / p.A-11.

**MFPT, opcode 000007:**

- R0 ← 4. UG §6.3.6 / p.6-63 says "the number 4 is placed in R0", ES p.22
  "processor code is 4", and UG §B.3.1.1 / p.B-4 "placed in the low byte
  of R0".
- Condition codes are not affected.

**PSW** (UG Fig. 1-3 / p.1-3):

- Bits <15:8> read as 0. <7:5> priority, 4 T, 3 N, 2 Z, 1 V, 0 C.
- There is no PSW at address 177776: MFPS and MTPS are the only access.
  The address 177776, like 177700-177717, is ordinary memory.
  (UG p.B-5, §B.5.2; ES p.C-2 item 7, p.C-4 item 18)
- **MTPS** loads PS<7:0> from the source byte but cannot set T. Its
  condition codes are set from the source (UG p.B-5).
- **MFPS** sets N from PS bit 7, sets Z if PS<7:0> = 0, clears V and leaves
  C (UG p.B-4).

**Not implemented** (opcodes trap to 10; UG Table B-2 / p.B-7; ES
p.C-6..C-7 items 31-34):

| Opcodes | What they are |
|---|---|
| 000010-000077 | reserved |
| 000210-000227 | reserved |
| 00023N | SPL |
| 0064NN | MARK |
| 0065SS | MFPI |
| 0066DD | MTPI |
| 007000-007777 | reserved |
| 070RSS-073RSS | MUL, DIV, ASH, ASHC |
| 07500R-07503R | FADD, FSUB, FMUL, FDIV |
| 075040-076777 | unused |
| 1065SS | MFPD |
| 1066DD | MTPD |
| 170000-177777 | FPP |

**Behaviour that differs from other PDP-11s** (UG §B.2 / p.B-1..B-3; ES p.C-1):

- **Same register as source (mode 0) and destination in mode 2, 3, 4 or 5**
  (`OPR R,(R)+`, `-(R)`, `@(R)+`, `@-(R)`): the register is incremented or
  decremented by 2 **before** it is used as the source operand. Examples:
  `MOV R0,(R0)+` with R0=1000 stores 1002; `MOV R0,-(R0)` stores 776.
- **PC as source with an index or relative destination**
  (`OPR PC,X(R)`, `@X(R)`, `A`, `@A`): the source value is the address of
  the opcode + 4.
- **`JMP (R)+` and `JSR reg,(R)+`:** the new PC is the initial contents of R.
- **SWAB** clears V.
- **MOV, CLR and SXT to memory** do a DATI, then a DATO; every DATO is
  preceded by a DATI. UG §B.4 warns that read-sensitive devices such as the
  8251 RxRDY are disturbed by this.
- **RESET:** -BCLR for at least 84T (ES) or 8.4 µs (UG, ES p.C-3), then an
  ASPI. A power fail during it waits until it ends.
- **WAIT:** loops pulsing PI (ASPI) until a request is seen. It is exited by
  a T trap.
- **HALT:** a trap through RESTART (§7.4).
- **Interrupt levels:** 4, coded on 4 lines (ES p.C-5 item 19). There are 8
  general-purpose registers. There is no stack-overflow trap (item 20).

## 10. Instruction timing

### 10.1 Units and composition

- **Time per instruction.** Static timing is
  `microcycles × 3T` (ES p.D-2; ES p.A-1 notes, where 1 µc = 0.4 µs at
  7.5 MHz). On top of that:
  - +T for each refresh performed (dynamic modes; §5.4);
  - +3T for each READY pulse (4T in long mode);
  - +T for each microcycle in long-microcycle mode.
  The φD of ASPI, IACK and DMA appear as "extra phases" in the latency
  table (ES p.B-7 note 0) and are not in the App. B counts.
- **Inside an instruction** (UG Table A-3 / p.A-4):
  - A bus read (`R`) or write (`W`) is 2 microcycles.
  - `Ref` (a refresh, or a bus NOP in static mode), `N` (bus NOP), `A`
    (ASPI) and `I` (IACK) are 1 microcycle each.
  - Every instruction starts with the fetch `R`, followed by a `Ref`/`N`
    slot (the decode).
  - [derived] For each instruction, the number of non-bus microcycles is the
    App. B total minus 2 × the App. E I/O count. It is given in the
    `nb16` column below.
- **Execution time is fixed:** whether a branch or SOB is taken does not
  change it (ES p.B-4..B-5 notes).
- **ES App. A / UG Tables A-17..A-21** give the same times in µs at 7.5 MHz,
  with "refresh on min/max" columns. In 16-bit mode, max − min ≤ 1T per
  expected refresh.

### 10.3 Order of transactions inside an instruction (UG Table A-3 / p.A-4)

Key: `R` read, `W` write, `Ref` refresh (a bus NOP in static mode), `N` bus
NOP, `A` ASPI, `I` IACK, `D` DMA. `X-Y` means indivisible.

| Instruction | 16-bit | 8-bit |
|---|---|---|
| CLR R0 | R Ref N | R-R Ref N |
| CLR (R0); MOV R0,(R1); MOV R0,(R1)+ | R Ref R-W | R-R Ref R-R-W-W |
| MOV R0,-(R1) | R Ref N R-W | R-R Ref N R-R-W-W |
| MOV R0,@X(R1) | R Ref R N R-W Ref† | R-R Ref R-R N R-R-W-W Ref† |
| MTPS R0 | R Ref N N N N N | R-R Ref N N N N N |
| JMP (R0) | R Ref N N | R-R Ref N N |
| JSR R0,(R1) | R Ref N N N N N | R-R Ref N N W-W N N |
| WAIT | R [Ref N A]... | R-R [Ref N A]... |
| HALT | R Ref N N W Ref N W N N A N | R-R Ref N N W-W Ref N W-W N N A N |
| EMT | R Ref N N W Ref N W R R N | R-R Ref N N W-W Ref N W-W R-R R N |
| RESET | R Ref N N N N [N N N]x9 N N N A N | R-R Ref N N N N [N N N]x9 N N N A N |
| interrupt | ...R-W [I N N* N W Ref N W R R N] R... | ...R-R-W-W [I N N* N W-W Ref N W-W R-R R N] R-R... |
| DMA | ...R-W D R... | ...R-R-W-W D R-R... |

Notes on the table:

- † absent in static mode.
- `[...]` in WAIT repeats until a request arrives; in RESET it repeats 9
  times with -BCLR low.
- `N*` is absent with an internal vector.
- The interrupt row starts with the last transaction of the interrupted
  instruction and ends with the handler's first fetch.

**WAIT loop:**

- ES p.B-6 note 2 calls it a 2-microinstruction loop, 2⅓ with refresh.
- ES p.A-7 note 2 and UG p.A-19 give 1.2 µs (= 3 µc), or 1.33 µs with
  refresh.
- UG A-3 shows 3 transactions per loop, `Ref N A`.

## 11. Other facts a pin model needs

- **Long-microcycle mode (MR1=0)** (ES long-microcycle sheet, PDF p.52):
  - Every microcycle gets φD.
  - The widths of -RAS, -CAS and PI grow by T each.
  - PI L.E. comes ≥ 2T-84 before the L.E. of pulse COUT (tMPO), or ≥ T-84
    before the T.E. of constant COUT (tLPO).
- **Output loading:** timings are specified at 80 pF. Correction:
  +0.3 ns/pF up to 200 pF, −0.3 ns/pF down to 25 pF (ES p.31 note 2).
- **Measurement levels:**

  | Pins | VOL | VOH |
  |---|---|---|
  | -RAS, -CAS, R/-WxB | 0.8 V | 2.4 V |
  | AI | 0.8 V | 2.2 V |
  | all others | 0.8 V | 2.0 V |

- **8-bit word access at an odd address:** not given. In 16-bit mode, a
  word access ignores A0 (UG §B.5.1).
- **Stack:** grows down from 376 after power-up. Nothing warns when SP goes
  below 377 (octal, as printed) into the vectors (UG §1.4, §B.5.1).
- **КР1807ВМ1** (outside these documents; already in `sources.md`): one
  catalogue gives the pin functions as the T-11's and a 7.6 MHz maximum
  clock.

## Not given by the documents

The model will have to determine these otherwise: from the board, the
emulator, measurement of a real КР1807ВМ1, or a choice recorded as such.

1. **Edges within a phase.** Which XTL edge (rising or falling, or a
   half-period point) moves each output, and so where inside its phase each
   strobe changes. Only drawings and min/max AC bounds exist, and several
   bounds (for example tRAS = 4T+35) imply edges off phase boundaries.
2. **Microcycle boundaries.** Where a microcycle formally begins: the
   phases are named φ1, φ2, (φD), φW, but every sheet draws transactions
   from φ2 to φ1.
3. **The microcode.** The sequence of microcycles for most instructions:
   only totals (App. B), I/O counts (App. E) and eleven example sequences
   (A-3) exist. Where the non-bus microcycles fall between the bus
   transactions is not given, nor the order of source and destination
   reads for most modes, nor which vector word is read first.
4. **The refresh scheduler in 16-bit mode.** Which opportunity refreshes
   ("every other"), the initial counter value, and how a refresh becomes a
   4T microcycle in place of a 3T NOP.
5. **The exact sampling instant of READY.** The internal latch of an edge,
   and how a pulse that straddles phases is counted.
6. **The interrupt latch.** Which PI's sample is acted on when several
   reads in one instruction see different request states. The arbitration
   point relative to the last transaction. The internal timing of the -PF
   "pause up to one instruction" and of the pseudo-edge detectors.
7. **Mode-register capture.** The exact instant or instants (the nine
   windows of UG Fig. A-16), and what the chip does with the pins in the
   ~150T between -BCLR negation and the first fetch beyond "10/20 refreshes
   or NOPs, register loads, ASPI". What AI and DAL drive between the
   power-up refreshes.
8. **State while PUP is high.** Whether the internal clock is fully stopped,
   and COUT's level.
9. **8-bit mode details.** Word accesses at odd addresses.
10. **MFPT** contents of R0<15:8> (UG says "R0", and also "low byte of R0").
11. **External vector with DAL undriven.** The "default vector 0" of UG
    Table A-14 against the "4..374" range of ES p.C-7.
12. **The conflicts above.** Each needs a decision, by software on the
    board (ROM-B self-test, the emulator in lockstep) where possible.
13. **КР1807ВМ1 against the T-11.** Any difference in timing or behaviour;
    the ТУ is wanted (`findings.md`).
